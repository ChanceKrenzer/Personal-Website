Making the Parts

CWRU shops, Spring 2026 to present

Knowing what a mill can reach, where a print will split and how long a second setup takes changes what I draw in CAD. I made most of the parts on this site myself.

Turning, milling and tapping

I machined a steel hammer from stock. I turned the handle and its grip grooves on the manual lathe, milled the head, drilled and tapped the 1/4-20 joint, and engraved the initials on a CNC mill. The handle diameter and head width were held to ±0.010 in. and checked with go/no-go gauges.

Planning the second operation

The VTOL landing gear brackets came off the waterjet with a tapered, rough wall, so I milled the bolt holes to size and tapped the threaded ones afterwards. Deciding up front which features the waterjet finishes and which need a second setup changes how the part is drawn.

The landing gear redesign

Joining

I bond parts with epoxy in alignment jigs I design and build. Where a printed part carries load, I use heat set inserts and bolts rather than screws threaded straight into plastic. The printed VTOL bracket that failed in flight is why.

I'm shop trained on MIG and TIG welding but haven't used either on flight hardware.

Printing and forming

Every project here uses FDM printing. I've also thermoformed the quadcopter's clear nose cone over a printed mold, used the laser cutter, and done some Mastercam for CNC setups.
